# Superseded textured TV experiment — 4 November 2026

This record describes the earlier textured-screen experiment. The shipped native display uses a single visible Studio webpage anchored to a flat physical face inside the existing housing. Its measurements are recorded separately.

## What changed

The in-world TV is one depth-tested textured surface on the original curved mesh. The native Studio iframe owns input and fullscreen state. Camera and avatar movement do not capture the UI or upload a texture. The previous sixteen projected copies, their overlap and their animation mirrors have been removed. The same texture is available to reflection cameras. Returning to the TV retains the native iframe, film selection and unfinished form.

Studio content changes produce a cached canvas. Finite browse transitions move cached card artwork with the source Motion transforms, and complex panels settle through a full-document paint. Hiding the TV stops paint scheduling. Unsupported capture can still open the real Studio. Ambient glints are sampled when the UI paints. Software composition of a complex state can take hundreds of milliseconds, during which the last complete image stays on the TV.

## Reproduction and checks

The original 526-frame, 30 fps recording included single-frame black strips. At 13.200 s most of the Aurelle poster disappeared. At 13.900 s a black column cut the title and card, and cleared one frame later.

The replacement was exercised in the Codex browser through normal Explore entry and player movement. On a production candidate, a supplemental moving third-person route produced 318 recorded frames, 262 with sufficient unobstructed, bright TV area for UV-based comparison. No broad missing columns were detected in those frames. A close first-person route at 0.344–0.969 m produced 179 frames, of which 141 met the same visibility criterion with no broad missing columns. Capture gaps and avatar occlusion limit this evidence.

The RTX 4070 Studio interaction checks covered wheel and keyboard browsing, activating a card, search, scroll, expand/return and hidden/resumed painting. Cached intermediate draws generally took 0.5–0.7 ms of JavaScript draw work. That is not a whole-frame timing. The live TV remains a 1600-pixel-wide source, so extremely close views have finite resolution.

## Verified work and storage reductions

- One final HDR/temporal submission follows the first live reflection instead of drawing once before it and once afterward. The same 22 opening reflection objects compile in one preparation call. This reduces repeated scene traversal. It does not eliminate their 22 material variants.
- Three scalar textures use R8 instead of RGBA8 on compatible backends. Measured texture allocation for those maps fell from 191.95 to 47.99 MiB. Original samples and actual stone/bronze material output matched on WebGPU and WebGL2. The unchanged source image path is available if the compact format cannot be used.
- Nine unique PNG responses totalled 17,693,714 bytes. Their lossless WebP representations total 8,509,712 bytes, 9,184,002 fewer. PNG fallback and all twelve GLB/character Brotli, gzip and identity responses passed production HTTP verification. Model transfer is still 257,962,361 bytes over Brotli.
- Inspection-only profiler and material review code load on request. Switching diagnostics off no longer overwrites a TV material installed since inspection began.
- Production build and 293 regression tests passed. Generated delivery files are created by the build and are not committed. The protected original workspace and Blender source were not edited.

Device coverage includes real RTX 4070 Chromium rendering and both graphics backends. A separate physical low-end PC was unavailable. The full lounge still requires all rooms and their lighting before Ready. These changes do not establish an 80% site-wide gain.

On the recorded walking/sprinting route with two timed runs after warmup (RTX 4070, WebGPU, High, 1280×720, recorder disabled), the published 5bebf86 baseline had a p95 frame-callback interval of 48.6 ms in both runs. The final localhost build recorded 30.3 and 24.3 ms. Across both runs the pooled p95 fell from 48.6 to 24.3 ms and intervals over 50 ms fell from 33/994 to 0/1,977. Callback throughput rose from 34.5/s to 67.7/s on this route. These are browser callback measurements on one PC, not certified display FPS or a site-wide speed percentage. Public and local cold-start timings are not a matched network comparison.
